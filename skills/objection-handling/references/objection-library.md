# Objection library

Each entry: what they say, the clarifying question, and two response variants. Adapt the wording to your voice and proof points.

## Brush-offs

**"Not interested."**
- Clarify: "Fair enough. Is it the timing, or does this just not apply to you?"
- A: "Totally fine. Most [titles] I talk to aren't, until [specific problem] shows up. Is that on your radar at all?"
- B (email): "Understood, I'll close the loop. If [trigger] ever becomes a priority, this is what we'd look at first: [one line]."

**"Just send me an email."**
- Clarify: "Sure. What would make it worth reading for you?"
- A: "Will do. If it's relevant, can we pencil 15 minutes on Thursday so it doesn't get buried?"

**"I'm busy."**
- A: "I'll be quick. 20 seconds on why I called, and you tell me if it's worth another minute?"

## Status quo

**"We already use X for this."**
- Clarify: "How long have you been on X? What made you choose it?"
- A: "Makes sense. Teams usually come to us from X when they hit [specific limit]. Have you run into that?"

**"It works fine."**
- Clarify: "What does fine look like in numbers?"
- A: "Good to hear. If it got 20% faster, would anyone notice, or is it really not a priority?"

## Price

**"Too expensive."**
- Clarify: "Compared to what?"
- A (value gap): Re-anchor on the cost of the problem they named.
- B (budget gap): "If price were off the table, would this be the right solution?" If yes, it's a structure conversation (phasing, scope, terms).

**"Your competitor is cheaper."**
- Clarify: "Are you comparing the same scope?"
- A: Compare total cost: implementation, admin time, add-ons, and what failure costs.

**"Can you do a discount?"**
- See pricing-negotiation. Never discount without getting something back.

## Timing

**"Not now, maybe next quarter."**
- Clarify: "What changes next quarter?"
- A: "Got it. What would need to be true then? I'll put a note to reach out on [date]."

**"We're in the middle of a reorg / migration."**
- A: "Makes sense to wait. When it wraps, is this something the new structure would own?"

## Authority

**"I need to talk to my boss."**
- Clarify: "Of course. What do you think they'll ask?"
- A: "Want to do it together? I can join for 15 minutes and answer the hard questions so it's not on you."
- B: Offer a one-page summary written for the boss, in the boss's metrics.

**"Procurement handles this."**
- A: "Great, what does their process look like and how long does it usually take?"

## Competitor

**"We're evaluating X too."**
- Clarify: "What's on your shortlist of must-haves?"
- A: Differentiate on their criteria only. Plant a landmine question (see competitive-battlecard).

**"We chose X."**
- A: "Congrats on making a decision. Out of curiosity, what tipped it? ... Mind if I check in after 90 days to see how the rollout went?"

## Build

**"We'll build it ourselves."**
- Clarify: "Who would own it after it's live?"
- A: Build-vs-buy table: build cost, maintenance (usually 15-20% of build cost a year), opportunity cost of engineers, time to value.

## Trust and risk

**"You're too small."**
- A: "Fair concern. Here's what we do to de-risk it: [reference customers], [SLA], [data export / exit clause]."

**"We tried something like this and it failed."**
- Clarify: "What went wrong?"
- A: Address the specific failure mode, then offer a scoped pilot with exit criteria.
